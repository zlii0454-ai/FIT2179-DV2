Australia's International Student Landscape

Who comes to Australia, where they study, and why international education matters.

This visualisation explores the changing scale, origin, sector distribution, and geographic concentration of international students in Australia. It focuses not only on where students come from, but also on how different Australian states and territories attract different student communities.

Part 1 · Growth over time

Australia’s international education system expanded rapidly before 2020

The first two charts show how international education grew over time and which education sectors shaped that growth.

International student enrolments grew strongly before 2020

Australia’s international student enrolments increased from about 275,000 in 2002 to more than 950,000 in 2019, before falling in 2020.

Higher education became the dominant international student sector

International student enrolments are not evenly distributed across education sectors. Higher education accounts for the largest share, while VET and ELICOS also played important roles in Australia’s international education system.

Part 2 · Source countries

Australia’s international student population is shaped by a small number of major source countries

The next charts compare national source-country patterns with sector composition and state-level differences.

Where do international students come from?

Australia’s international student population is strongly shaped by a small number of major source countries. China and India remain the two largest contributors, followed by Nepal and several Southeast Asian countries.

Higher education dominated the 2020 sector composition

By 2020, higher education remained the largest part of Australia’s international education system. This radial chart summarises the sector composition in a single year.

Part 3 · State differences

Different states attract different international student communities

State-level patterns reveal that international student communities are not evenly distributed across Australia.

Different states attract different student communities

The distribution of source countries varies across Australian states and territories. This heatmap highlights how major student communities are concentrated differently across the country.

Which states rely most on international students?

Larger states generally host more international students, but the relationship is not only about population size. Comparing student numbers with state population helps reveal where international students form a larger share of the community.

Some states host more international students relative to their population

A state with more international students does not always have the highest student concentration. By comparing international students with total population, this map shows where international students form a larger share of the local community.

Part 4 · Local profiles and concentration

Looking beyond totals reveals more detailed student patterns

The final charts use rankings and small multiples to compare state-level student profiles in more detail.

Source-country rankings shift across states

The largest source countries are not ranked the same way in every state. This ranking flow chart shows how major student communities change position across Australian states and territories.

Which state has the highest international student concentration?

Total student numbers can favour larger states, so this ranking compares international students relative to each state or territory’s population. This reveals where international students form a larger share of the local community.

Each state has its own top source-country profile

Looking at each state separately reveals local differences that are hidden in national totals. While China and India are major source countries overall, some states show stronger concentrations of students from Nepal, Vietnam, or Bangladesh.

Data sources: Australian Government Department of Education international student data, Australian Bureau of Statistics population data, and Australian state boundary GeoJSON.
