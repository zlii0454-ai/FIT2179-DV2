Australia's International Student Landscape

Who comes to Australia, where they study, and why international education matters.

This visualisation explores the changing scale, origin, sector distribution, and geographic concentration of international students in Australia. It focuses not only on where students come from, but also on how different Australian states and territories attract different student communities.

Summary · Key insights

International education in Australia is large, concentrated, and unevenly distributed

1. Growth was strong before 2020

International student enrolments increased substantially from 2002 to 2019, before declining in 2020 during border disruption.

2. Source countries are highly concentrated

China and India remain the two largest source countries, while Nepal, Vietnam, Bangladesh and other Asian countries also form major student communities.

3. States attract different communities

New South Wales and Victoria host large international student populations, but each state and territory has its own source-country profile.

4. Relative concentration tells a different story

When adjusted by population, smaller jurisdictions can show a high international student concentration even if their total student numbers are lower.

Data sources: Australian Government Department of Education international student data, Australian Bureau of Statistics population data, and Australian state boundary GeoJSON.
